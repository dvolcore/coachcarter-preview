The objective and the judgment

  • Objective: build the Coach Carter brand into a durable global franchise. The target is the brand and the business around it, rather than Coach's personal net worth.
  • Judgment: pursue this seriously. A recognizable story, a real founder, a clear code and a new Academy generation create a credible franchise opportunity.
  • A $500M or $1B outcome requires a large, repeatable commercial ecosystem. No public evidence establishes that valuation today or a reliable probability of reaching it.

The decisions to understand first (the playbook lists these under "six decisions"; seven bullets appear)

  • Lead with Academy. New students, parents, rivals and alumni can carry recurring stories. Coach supplies the standard; new characters make the world expandable.
  • Use the documentary as a major launch asset. The real man, present-day conflict and corroborated untold history give audiences another reason to care. A streamer is one possible buyer, alongside distributors, sponsors, screenings and publishing.
  • Sell entertainment with a business purpose. A brand can fund a story arc plus community activation. This is already happening in the market; Carter's actual price still needs a paid test.
  • Build beyond his calendar. Catalog licenses, local adaptations, partner clubs, useful products and character spin-offs can earn without Coach appearing personally every time.
  • Build the whole franchise. Stories, catalog, clubs, academies and consumer licenses create the commercial ecosystem. Value its earnings first; use standalone brand valuation as a component analysis.
  • Price the web series; give away only the preview. Proposed launch: $19.99 one-time Season One access. Full episodes behind paid access; the public site gets a separate 60-second preview. Checkout is not yet live.
  • Make the next decision a paid proof. Finish and assess the existing episode, sell a scoped continuation, reconcile delivered cost, and measure whether viewers and buyers return.

05 · Ch 17

The first 90 days, the $60K test and the four scale gates

Why it's here, not on the public site: Internal budget, release conditions and sales targets — advice for Coach and Ralph, not a sales message.

The key points
  • $60,000 is a proposed planning cap, not authorization to spend; it excludes a full documentary or scripted season.
  • Money is released in three steps: up to $15K, then $20K, then $25K — each only after a gate is met.
  • Gate to the second step: three signed pilot orders with deposits collected, or a signed sponsor with first payment collected.
  • Interview 20 buyers in the first 30 days and sell no more than two offers first.
  • Free praise and nonbinding enthusiasm do not count; only booked, invoiced and collected money counts.
  • A $500M/$1B valuation conversation only becomes real at Gate 4, after durable growth and meaningful earnings.

Days 1–30: establish the baseline and sell (release up to $15,000)

  • Assemble financials, analytics, customer history, existing books/programs, and the current episode.
  • Interview 20 buyers: five brand/agency content buyers, five corporate workforce/community buyers, five school/youth buyers, and five sports/media buyers.
  • Compare three offers: the sponsored Academy package, the limited Coach-led video show, and a practical institutional program. Select no more than two to sell first.
  • Create the $19.99 planned season offer with a free 60-second preview; validate protected paid access before sales.
  • Record current Coach material, show the existing episode to target viewers, and request scoped production bids.
  • Test three Academy hook concepts and a coherent short arc; measure whether new characters retain viewers beyond recognition of Coach.

Gate 1 → release of the next tranche

  • Seek three signed pilot orders with deposits collected, or a signed sponsor commitment with an initial payment collected.
  • Track booked, invoiced, and collected amounts separately.
  • Free praise and nonbinding enthusiasm do not meet the gate.
  • If the offers fail, change the buyer, proposition, price, or delivery before increasing production spending.

Days 31–60: deliver and measure (release up to $20,000 after the gate)

  • Deliver the funded pilot: either a scoped content package with a production partner or a practical program with a qualified educator/facilitator.
  • For content, track completion, return viewing, distribution delivery, and the sponsor objective.
  • For programs, track participation, implementation, and buyer feedback.
  • Record direct costs and contribution for both.
  • Track baseline and follow-up without claiming that a small uncontrolled pilot proves causal academic improvement.
  • Assess the Academy cut and make a focused documentary treatment/proof plan.

Days 61–90: earn the next commitment (release up to $25,000 after delivery evidence)

  • Seek five total paid pilots and three speaking engagements; test actual offers and prices rather than assuming them.
  • Target: five $5,000 pilots plus three $15,000 speeches = $70,000 in gross bookings. It is a sales target, not promised revenue, collected cash, or profit.
  • In a sponsor-led path, deliver within the signed scope and seek a second season, renewal, or additional distribution partner.
  • Do not add the illustrative $75,000 package to the program-led bookings target as if both were certain.
  • Seek paid extensions and next-term commitments. Three of five buyers expressing renewal intent would be encouraging but is weaker evidence than actual paid renewal.
  • Scale recurring licenses after the first renewal cohort, not before.
  • Evaluate the present-day documentary proof and the strongest scripted concept with intended viewers and qualified buyers/operators.
  • Pursue one lead entertainment package with clear next steps. A full slate can remain on the opportunity board without being financed simultaneously.
