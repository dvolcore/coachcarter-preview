01 — The thesis in 60 seconds
Why invest in Coach Carter

Fame gets the meeting.
Ownership makes the money.

01

A proven, global story

#1 at the U.S. box office in 2005. Netflix Global Top 10 in 2022 and 2025, and 38.5 million Netflix viewing hours in the second half of 2025. FIBA Foundation featured Coach Carter material in a Swiss basketball exhibition in January 2026. No sequel or series has ever been made.

02

No measured path to the brand

A dated website with no priced institutional program, newsletter funnel or outcome case studies; no owned or regular podcast (only guest appearances); and bureau-quoted keynotes of $10–30K while some true-story peers list $30–200K (bureau listings, not completed deals). Studios, streamers and clip pages carry his scenes. Coach's own analytics and financials have not yet been measured — that baseline is Phase 0.

03

The market is buying this exact shape

Legacy revivals (Cobra Kai, Creed, Bel-Air, A Different World), networks commissioning vertical drama, and investors funding sports properties — the Unrivaled league at a $650M Series C valuation.

04

An edge no one else has

The real man and his approval, a film audiences keep returning to, an existing Coach Carter Academy episode to finish and test with target viewers, and AI-assisted production we will prove by cost per accepted finished minute.

05

A gated path from proof to a franchise

Today's value is unestablished. The three-year planning scenario — $15–25M revenue, a $24–60M company at 8–12× EBITDA — is a case to test, and the $500M and $1B targets are earned gate by gate with paid proof.

04 — The gap
Where the value leaks today

Top-tier fame.
Bottom-tier pricing.

Coach Carter belongs to the same rare group as Rudy, The Blind Side and The Pursuit of Happyness: real people whose lives became hit films. No independent ranking measures his brand against theirs — but the pricing gap is measurable.

Keynote fees: Coach Carter vs. comparable true-story names

Published speaker-bureau ranges, thousands of U.S. dollars per keynote

Coach Carter rows: advertised quotes from All American Speakers, Executive Speakers and Gotham Artists; one agency also lists $5–10K virtual. Peer ranges: bureau listings compiled in the Coach Carter Brand Valuation Playbook (Nov 2026). Advertised estimates, not verified completed deals.

Who profits now

  • The studio's clip channels and the streamers carry the scenes.
  • Quote pages, fan channels and "Coach Carter speech" pages monetize his story.
  • Speaker bureaus repost the film to sell bookings quoted at $10–30K.
  • We found no owned audience list or recurring product in public sources; Coach's analytics and financials are the Phase 0 baseline.

How the ceiling breaks

  • Speaking is finite. It is a real market with limited personal capacity: at an assumed $20K fee, 20 engagements gross $400K before commissions, travel, staff and tax.
  • Ownership compounds. Talent-led companies are valued as businesses — a 2021 investment valued SpringHill at $725M (later combined with Fulwell 73).
  • The brand becomes a company — a values studio plus programs and properties. Ownership terms will be set at formation and disclosed in the data room.

The market is moving toward this exact shape

The revival

The revivals that became franchises landed 29–39 years after the original — a legacy figure mentoring a new generation. Coach Carter is 21 years old, and its 25th anniversary, January 2030, is the launch moment.

    07 — The strategy
    One umbrella, three engines

    A franchise,
    not a personality.

    The story makes the standard famous; distribution brings viewers back; participation turns the standard into something people can join and buy. Buyers pay premiums only when each release makes the next one cheaper to make, easier to distribute or more valuable to license — that is what we prove.

    Story engine

    The Coach Carter universe

    • Coach Carter Academy — the lead recurring series, a new generation
    • The Real Coach Carter — documentary for truth, credibility and a coordinated launch
    • New characters earn their own spin-offs through audience response
    • "Coach Carter Presents" — later, a wider catalog
    Distribution engine

    How viewers find it and return

    • Short clips introduce a specific conflict
    • Full arcs create return viewing
    • Existing distributors and local partners expand reach
    • An opt-in Carter audience gives direct feedback for the next release
    • Vertical episodes and longer compilations edited as separate products
    Participation engine

    What people can join and buy

    • The Contract — six-week team program and school program
    • Events and team challenges: the Coach Carter Classic
    • Partner-delivered programs and clubs run by qualified facilitators
    • An approved voice/narration library, tested with one paying buyer first
    • Contract products, apparel, signed editions

    Participation products are optional, with separate value — not tolls inserted into every story. Parents, coaches and adult fans may be the buyers even when young people are the viewers.

    Comparable outcomes

    Company value vs. brand value

    The $1B target is what a buyer would pay for the franchise — IP, properties and contracts. A standalone trademark is narrower: at a 5% royalty, 12% discount rate and 3% growth, a $1B trademark needs about $2.4B a year of branded sales across licensees. We report the two separately and never add them together.

    Timing and ownership

    A $1B value reached in year ten is worth about $322M today at a 12% discount rate, so we raise only after each proof point. Ownership terms will be set at formation and disclosed in the data room. Enterprise value does not equal equity proceeds.

    Own it · prove it · sell it · scale it

    Four phases.
    Hard gates.

    13 — Risks & diligence
    What can sink it — and what we're doing about it

    Clear eyes.
    Clean books.

    Key personMost of the value sits with one founder. → Build a bench (former players, family and trained, quality-checked facilitators) and IP that outlives him.
    Entity clean-upThe IRS revoked the Coach Ken Carter Foundation's exemption in 2010 (Announcement 2010-79, verified); current status of the Foundation and the Texas Academy entity is unresolved and not independently confirmed. → Match today's names and identifiers to official records in Phase 0, before any closing.
    Film rightsParamount owns the 2005 film. → The company trades on Coach's name, likeness, story and new IP; film material only through official embeds or licenses. The original Contract has not been located in an authenticated signed scan; Coach's own account calls it a nineteen-point pledge, and we will not publish clauses we cannot source.
    CredibilityOnly verified facts are pitched, and known errors in the public record are being corrected.
    Micro-drama cyclePlatforms come and go. → Brands fund the seasons; we never carry app risk.
    AI backlash→ Every release leads with Coach's consent, his real voice and his on-camera endorsement.
    Name lending→ Equity plus approval rights, or no deal. Never sell the name.
    Bandwidth & competition→ An operator runs the machine; speed matters — the basketball-mentorship lane is filling (a youth-basketball vertical series, I Am Hoop, was announced without a disclosed platform or fee).
    Recognition is not demandPeople who know the movie, people who know Ken Carter and people who will pay are three different groups. → Moving the first to the third is the business; a viral scene can open the door but does not prove conversion.
    Creative riskFamiliarity does not remove creative risk; a revival announcement or a pile of scripts is not asset value. → Every format has a named failure mode and a viewer test before it scales.
    Media concentrationA media-led route concentrates risk in fewer buyers and needs working capital. → Keep programs and licensing alongside production; track cash timing, not headline budgets.
    ValuationToday's value is unestablished, and the multiples used are selected sensitivities. → A small, founder-dependent business could trade far lower, and a buyer may not exist at any modeled price.

    We stop or redesign when…

    • Buyers repeatedly will not pay after the offer and buyer segment have been tested.
    • Delivery depends on Coach doing every task or erodes program quality.
    • Audience acquisition and production costs exceed the contribution they generate.
    • A project keeps consuming money without stronger buyer commitments or viewer retention.
    • Merchandise inventory, an app or a physical academy is proposed before demand supports it.

    What would change the assessment upward

    A verified, engaged owned audience; profitable booked demand; a repeatable curriculum; actual institutional renewals; a committed sponsor; an entertainment commission or distribution agreement with favorable economics; and a management team capable of operating beyond Coach's calendar. Another unfinanced sequel announcement would not.
