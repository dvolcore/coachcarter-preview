COACH CARTER ACADEMY · TEAM PACK

Read. Reflect.
Raise the standard.

Facilitator guide for a small-group reading and action session. Designed for one facilitator and up to 25 participants with a copy of the published book.

Session outcome
Each participant leaves with one specific behavior to practice, one person to check in with, and a completed Commitment Card. The goal is useful conversation and follow-through—not a test of recall.

Before the room opens

  1. Place one book and one two-sided Commitment Card at each seat.
  2. Prepare a visible timer, pens, and a wall or board for shared themes.
  3. Tell the group that people may pass on any question. No one needs to disclose private information.
  4. Choose a 30-day check-in date before the session begins.

SESSION PLAN

60-minute run of show

00–05 · Welcome and frame

Explain the outcome. Invite participants to listen for decisions, habits, and consequences that connect to their own lives.

05–15 · Quiet reading

Give everyone ten minutes to revisit a section they selected. Ask them to mark an idea that challenged, clarified, or motivated them. Do not require anyone to read aloud.

15–30 · Triads

In groups of three, use the prompts below. One person speaks, one listens for a concrete action, and one watches the time. Rotate roles.

30–42 · Whole-group harvest

Collect themes on the board under standard, obstacle, and support. Keep contributions brief and make room for different perspectives.

42–52 · Write the card

Participants complete the back of the Commitment Card: a practice, a first action, a check-in person, name, signature, and date. Encourage observable verbs: call, study, arrive, apologize, practice, finish.

52–60 · Close and assign the check-in

Invite volunteers to share only what they want to share. Collect no cards unless participants choose to share them. Confirm the 30-day check-in owner and method.

DISCUSSION TOOLKIT

Prompts that move from story to action

  1. What choice or standard stayed with you, and why?
  2. Where does a good intention become a daily practice?
  3. What pressure makes your standard hardest to keep?
  4. What support would make the next right action easier?
  5. What would someone else be able to observe if you followed through?
  6. What is the smallest credible first step within 48 hours?

Facilitator moves

  • Make it specific: ask “When, where, and how will that happen?”
  • Share airtime: use a round, pair-share, or a written response before open discussion.
  • Separate impact from intent: invite curiosity about outcomes without turning the session into a debate about character.
  • Protect the room: redirect personal attacks, keep examples voluntary, and avoid promising confidentiality you cannot provide.
  • Honor different starting points: the card is a personal commitment, not a ranking system.

30-day check-in

Use the same three questions by phone, in person, or in a private group:

  1. What did you practice?
  2. What got in the way?
  3. What is your next 7-day action?
