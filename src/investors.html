03 — The gap
Where the value leaks today

Top-tier fame.
Bottom-tier pricing.

Among real people whose lives became hit films, Coach Carter's recognition is top tier — alongside Rudy, The Blind Side and The Pursuit of Happyness. His pricing sits at the bottom.

Who profits now

  • The studio's clip channels and the streamers carry the scenes.
  • Quote pages, fan channels and "Coach Carter speech" pages monetize his story.
  • Speaker bureaus repost the film to sell bookings at $10–20K.
  • The brand owns no audience list and no recurring product.

How the ceiling breaks

  • Fees cap out. Speaking-only careers top out around $12–60M across every comparable.
  • Ownership compounds. Tyler Perry owns his library; Magic Johnson owns businesses; LeBron built SpringHill.
  • The brand becomes a company — a values studio plus programs and properties — and Coach keeps the majority.

Comparable outcomes

Company value vs. brand value

The $1B target is what a buyer would pay for the franchise — IP, properties and contracts. A standalone trademark is narrower: at a 5% royalty, 12% discount rate and 3% growth, a $1B trademark needs about $2.4B a year of branded sales across licensees. We report the two separately and never add them together.

Timing and ownership

A $1B value reached in year ten is worth about $322M today at a 12% discount rate, so we raise only after each proof point. Coach's ownership is locked at formation: at 60% of a $500M company, his stake would be $300M before taxes and costs.

Own it · prove it · sell it · scale it

Four phases.
Hard gates.

Targets: $4M+ annual revenue by the end of 2027, $15–25M by 2029, and the film's 25th anniversary — January 2030 — as the global moment.

Phase 0 · now → Dec 2026

Own it

  • Form Coach Carter Brands — one company owning name, likeness, voice, story and the Contract
  • Platform, store, list, socials — live in preview
  • Reconstitute the Foundation and Academy entities
  • Record Coach's archive on camera; ride the Paramount+ wave

Gate: verified accounts, working store, 10K emails, 10M+ clip views, a 12-month baseline

Phase 1 · Q1 2027

Prove it

  • Package Coach Carter II Episode 1 as the spec
  • Sell a brand a sponsored Academy season
  • Launch the weekly video podcast
  • $50–100K clipping campaign → 100M views; reprice keynotes to $30K+

Gate: one sponsor paid, 100M official views, 10 episodes, first keynote at the new rate

Phase 2 · Q2–Q4 2027

Sell it

Scale it

  • Camps → Clubs & Academy network with HBCU and international partners
  • Raise growth capital against real revenue
  • January 2030: documentary premiere, tour and season launch

Gate: $15–25M revenue — then build the full $1B stack

How capital goes in — and comes out

Raise against proof,
not promises.

Stage 1 · validation

$60K, in three tranches

$15K for the baseline and buyer interviews → $20K released after three paid pilots or an anchor sponsor's deposit → $25K released after delivery evidence and a paid renewal.

Stage 2 · operating build

Team, content, audience

An operator/GM, the podcast and clip engine, the documentary sample, programs and legal clean-up of the entities and trademarks. Sized in the data room against Phase 1 results.

Stage 3 · growth capital

Against collected revenue

Rights-focused funds are active — Shamrock closed an $813M content fund in 2026. Raised at the $15–25M revenue gate to build the Clubs, the universe and licensing.

Exit pathways — precedents, not promises

Strategic sale

Studios and media groups buy talent-led companies — SpringHill was valued at $725M.

Rights-fund recap

Content funds buy libraries and recurring licensing at premium multiples.

Property sale

Youth-sports platforms trade on cash flow — IMG Academy sold for $1.25B.

Public markets

Values-media and league precedents exist — Angel Studios (NYSE), BIG3 via SPAC. A long-run option at scale, not the plan.
