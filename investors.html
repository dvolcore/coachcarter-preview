01

Today's value is unestablished. The three-year planning scenario — $15–25M revenue, a $24–60M company at 8–12× EBITDA — is a case to test, and the $500M and $1B targets are earned gate by gate with paid proof.

03 — The gap
Where the value leaks today

Top-tier fame.
Bottom-tier pricing.

Coach Carter belongs to the same rare group as Rudy, The Blind Side and The Pursuit of Happyness: real people whose lives became hit films. No independent ranking measures his brand against theirs — but the pricing gap is measurable.

Keynote fees: Coach Carter vs. comparable true-story names

Coach Carter rows: advertised quotes from All American Speakers, Executive Speakers and Gotham Artists; one agency also lists $5–10K virtual. Peer ranges: bureau listings compiled in the Coach Carter Brand Valuation Playbook (Nov 2026). Advertised estimates, not verified completed deals.

Who profits now

  • The studio's clip channels and the streamers carry the scenes.
  • Quote pages, fan channels and "Coach Carter speech" pages monetize his story.
  • Speaker bureaus repost the film to sell bookings quoted at $10–30K.
  • The brand owns no audience list and no recurring product.

How the ceiling breaks

  • Speaking is finite. It is a real market with limited personal capacity: at an assumed $20K fee, 20 engagements gross $400K before commissions, travel, staff and tax.
  • Ownership compounds. Tyler Perry owns his library; Magic Johnson owns businesses; LeBron built SpringHill.
  • The brand becomes a company — a values studio plus programs and properties — and Coach keeps the majority.

    06 — The strategy
    One umbrella, three engines

    A franchise,
    not a personality.

    The story makes the standard famous; distribution brings viewers back; participation turns the standard into something people can join and buy. Buyers pay premiums only when each release makes the next one cheaper to make, easier to distribute or more valuable to license — that is what we prove.

    Story engine

    The Coach Carter universe

    • Coach Carter Academy — the lead recurring series, a new generation
    • The Real Coach Carter — documentary for truth, credibility and a coordinated launch
    • New characters earn their own spin-offs through audience response
    • "Coach Carter Presents" — later, a wider catalog
    Distribution engine

    How viewers find it and return

    • Short clips introduce a specific conflict
    • Full arcs create return viewing
    • Existing distributors and local partners expand reach
    • An opt-in Carter audience gives direct feedback for the next release
    • Vertical episodes and longer compilations edited as separate products
    Participation engine

    What people can join and buy

    • The Contract — six-week team program and school program
    • Events and team challenges: the Coach Carter Classic
    • Partner-delivered programs and clubs run by qualified facilitators
    • An approved voice/narration library, tested with one paying buyer first
    • Contract products, apparel, signed editions

    Participation products are optional, with separate value — not tolls inserted into every story. Parents, coaches and adult fans may be the buyers even when young people are the viewers.

    Comparable outcomes

    Company value vs. brand value

    The $1B target is what a buyer would pay for the franchise — IP, properties and contracts. A standalone trademark is narrower: at a 5% royalty, 12% discount rate and 3% growth, a $1B trademark needs about $2.4B a year of branded sales across licensees. We report the two separately and never add them together.

    Timing and ownership

    A $1B value reached in year ten is worth about $322M today at a 12% discount rate, so we raise only after each proof point. Coach's ownership is locked at formation; as an illustration only, 60% of a $500M company would be $300M before debt, taxes and costs.

    09 — The plan
    Own it · prove it · sell it · scale it

    Four phases.
    Hard gates.

    Each phase opens only when the one before it is paid for: paid repeatability, then an earning franchise, then diversified expansion. The film's 25th anniversary — January 2030 — is the global moment.

    Phase 0 · now → Dec 2026

    Own it

    • Form Coach Carter Brands — one company owning name, likeness, voice, story and the Contract
    • Platform, store, list, socials — live in preview
    • Reconstitute the Foundation and Academy entities
    • Record Coach's archive on camera; ride the Paramount+ wave

    Gate: a 12-month analytics and financial baseline, 20 buyer interviews, no more than two offers chosen to sell first

    Phase 1 · Q1 2027

    Prove it

    • Package Coach Carter Academy Episode 1 as the spec
    • Sell a brand a sponsored Academy season
    • Launch the weekly video podcast
    • Test speaking at real prices — the 90-day target assumes three $15K speeches, against bureau quotes of $10–30K

    Gate: paid repeatability — finished work, two independently paid cycles, reconciled unit costs and repeat viewers

    Phase 2 · Q2–Q4 2027

    Sell it

    • Shop the documentary and the series to streamers; vertical slate to networks and apps
    • Launch the Contract school program; test an approved voice/narration library with one paying buyer
    • Camps in 5–10 cities; first Coach Carter Classic
    • HBCU package around the Celebration Bowl

    Gate: an earning franchise — multiple buyers, a catalog renewal, product sell-through and a local operator who delivers without Coach

    Phase 3 · 2028–2030

    Scale it

    • Values studio: several brand-funded series a year, dubbed for Latin America and Europe
    • Camps → Clubs & Academy network with HBCU and international partners
    • Raise growth capital against a repeatable operation, not an idea slate
    • January 2030: documentary premiere, tour and season launch

    Gate: diversified expansion — repeated seasons, repeat license receipts, audited economics. A $500M/$1B conversation starts only with durable earnings and credible outside pricing

    10 — Capital & exit
    How capital goes in — and comes out

    Raise against proof,
    not promises.

    Stage 1 · validation

    $60K, in three tranches

    $15K for the baseline and buyer interviews → $20K released after three signed pilot orders with deposits, or a sponsor's initial payment → up to $25K released after delivery evidence, while we seek a paid renewal.

    Stage 2 · operating build

    Team, content, audience

    An operator/GM, the podcast and clip engine, the documentary sample, programs and legal clean-up of the entities and trademarks. Sized in the data room against Phase 1 results.

    Stage 3 · growth capital

    Against collected revenue

    Rights-focused funds are active — Shamrock closed an $813M content fund in 2026. Raised against a repeatable operation — repeated seasons, repeat license receipts and audited economics — to build the Clubs, the universe and licensing.

    Exit pathways — precedents, not promises

    Strategic sale

    Studios and media groups invest in talent-led companies — a 2021 investment valued SpringHill at $725M.

    Rights-fund recap

    Content funds buy libraries and recurring licensing at premium multiples.

    Property sale

    Sports-education operators trade on cash flow — IMG Academy was acquired at $1.25B enterprise value.

    Public markets

    Values-media and league precedents exist — Angel Studios (NYSE), BIG3 via SPAC. A long-run option at scale, not the plan.
